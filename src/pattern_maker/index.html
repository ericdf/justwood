Carver's Pattern Maker

Three steps: choose a photograph of your object and the shape is traced for you, re-arrange the pattern if you want to, then print it at whatever size you like.

Add more views — a top and a side, say. They scale together and share registration marks, so they line up easily on the blank.

A fourth, optional step — and the best one: chip in if Carver's Pattern Maker turns out to be useful.

Privacy, by design

Your photographs and your pattern never leave this device. Nothing is uploaded. No cookies, no trackers, no accounts.

Work in progress is held in your browser so a reload does not lose it, and "Start over" clears it — but save the PDF; it's what you came for.

We can see how many people visit, like any website, but nothing about who you are or what you made. If you decide to tip, that happens on Stripe's own site under their privacy policy — nothing here is loaded from them until you go.

Good to know

Stray shading away from the object is ignored — only the largest connected area becomes the pattern. Shading joined to the object does print, so erase that if you care to. Tidy it as much or as little as you like.

2 Lay out the pattern

Set the length — any size you like, from a teaspoon to an oar. Guides and layout lines annotate; make symmetrical reshapes the pattern.

The longest dimension your finished carving should be. Everything scales proportionally — width follows length and the shape is never stretched, so a 6" spoon and a 12" spoon are the same design at two sizes. Change it whenever you like and the pattern below redraws; the printed page is always true to your chosen measurement. One size applies to every pattern in the set, so they line up with each other on the blank.

Drag a line down the axis of symmetry on the pattern below, then pick which of the two results you want. One side is mirrored onto the other, so both match. A short stroke is enough — the whole axis is worked out from its direction.

Available once you have chosen a shape above. You will be able to show a centerline and registration marks, make the shape symmetrical, and draw layout lines on it.

Reference lines printed with the pattern to help you orient it on the blank — the grid for scale and the centerline for squaring it up. Turn off any you do not want; they are printed guidance, not part of the shape.

Registration marks are five ticks placed for you: both ends, the middle, and the two points halfway between. They sit at the same proportion of every view, so they stay put when you change the size. Line them up when transferring to the wood and the top and side views will agree.

Mirrors one side of your outline about an axis you choose, so both sides match. Draw the axis, then pick which of the two results you want. You can mirror again on a different axis to refine the shape. The traced shape is replaced — undo steps back one mirroring, and cancel keeps the original.

A line you strike against while carving. Drag across the pattern to draw one at any angle; nearly-square strokes snap square. Drag either end afterwards to adjust it. They print as dashed lines, darker than the guides, because they are meant to be worked to rather than just read.

Useful when a guide is not what you want: turn off the centerline above and draw your own where you think it belongs.
